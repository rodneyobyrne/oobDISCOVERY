# BUSINESS TYPE: clinician

## Purpose
Reusable preparation context for Discovery projects currently classified as `clinician`.

This is the initial business-type value inherited from the original oobDISCOVERY implementation. It should be refined or split into more precise types such as `behavioral-health`, `therapy-practice`, `recovery-services`, or another category when project evidence shows that a narrower classification produces better preparation.

## Current evidence status
The repository currently contains substantial **Varetto-specific** questionnaire, persona-study, service, and audience material. That material is project evidence, not proof that the same patterns apply to every clinician or clinical organization.

## Established / sourced knowledge
No cross-client business-type claims have yet been promoted to established reusable knowledge in this file.

## Repeated Discovery patterns
No pattern should be labeled repeated until it has been observed across more than one genuinely independent project and the relevant differences are recorded.

## Working hypotheses to test
- People seeking care may need clear service-fit and boundary information before they can decide whether to contact a provider.
- Trust, privacy, professional credibility, access, cost, timing, and uncertainty about what happens next may influence help-seeking decisions.
- The person receiving care may not be the only decision participant; partners, family members, referral sources, employers, or other stakeholders may influence the path depending on service type.
- Diagnostic labels alone are unlikely to provide enough information for communication strategy; situation, motivation, barriers, decision state, and language may be more useful dimensions to investigate.

These are **working hypotheses**, not universal truths.

## Likely intake pre-work
### Business model and service structure
- Identify actual service lines and what is / is not offered.
- Identify licensure, geography, payment, access, scheduling, and referral constraints relevant to the project.
- Distinguish clinical services from adjacent recovery, housing, coaching, education, or support services where applicable.

### Customer / audience situations to investigate
- Who is actively seeking help and who is influencing the decision?
- What change, consequence, concern, or threshold makes the person begin looking?
- What may delay contact even when need is recognized?
- What would indicate that this provider is or is not an appropriate fit?

### Decision and trust questions to investigate
- What information must be understood before first contact feels reasonable?
- What forms of credibility matter to this particular audience?
- What privacy, stigma, safety, or uncertainty concerns shape the decision?

### Common proof requirements to verify
- Credentials and scope of practice
- Actual services and populations served
- Access and scheduling realities
- Payment / insurance facts when relevant
- Referral and service boundaries
- Claims about outcomes, specialization, or treatment approach

## Known variations that should prevent overgeneralization
- therapy practice vs. recovery services vs. higher levels of care
- solo practice vs. group practice vs. facility / organization
- private pay vs. insurance-supported models
- local market and referral ecosystem
- population served and service specialization
- regulatory and licensure differences
- voluntary vs. externally influenced help-seeking

## Project-specific facts that must NOT be generalized
- Varetto Recovery personas, audience labels, service mix, language, and prior keyword study remain Varetto-specific unless later cross-project evidence supports a broader pattern.

## Open research gaps
- Which decision patterns repeat across multiple independent clinical projects?
- Which patterns are actually service-line-specific rather than industry-wide?
- Which trust signals measurably affect contact behavior by audience and market?
- Which assumptions fail when geography, payer model, demographics, or level of care changes?

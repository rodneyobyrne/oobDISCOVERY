## Working hypotheses to test
- Customer intent may differ substantially between urgent/emergency needs and planned installation, replacement, maintenance, or remodeling work.
- Geography and service area are likely to affect both operational fit and customer search behavior.
- Trust may depend on practical proof such as responsiveness, licensing/insurance where applicable, clear expectations, workmanship, reviews/reputation, and understandable pricing or estimate practices.
- Homeowners, property managers, builders, commercial buyers, and other customer types may have different decision criteria and buying cycles.
- The problem the customer describes may not use the same terminology the plumber uses internally.

These are hypotheses to test in each project, not universal truths.

## Likely intake pre-work
### Business model and service structure
- Identify residential, commercial, construction, maintenance, emergency, specialty, and recurring-service work actually offered.
- Identify service area and any dispatch/travel constraints.
- Identify how estimates, emergency fees, scheduling, warranties, memberships, financing, or payment work where relevant.
- Identify work the business does not accept.

### Customer / audience situations to investigate
- Immediate failure or damage
- Recurring problem that has become disruptive
- Planned repair or replacement
- Remodel or construction planning
- Preventive maintenance
- Property-management or multi-location needs
- Price-comparison or second-opinion situations

Each should be tested against the actual client rather than assumed.

### Decision and trust questions to investigate
- How quickly does the customer need help?
- What uncertainty makes them hesitate to call?
- What proof helps them believe the business can diagnose and solve the problem reliably?
- What information do they need about timing, access, disruption, price, guarantees, or next steps?
- Who besides the caller may influence the decision?

### Language / terminology to clarify
Capture both customer language and trade language. Do not assume the customer knows the technical name of a component, failure mode, or service.

### Research prompts
- Current local licensing and regulatory requirements
- Local service-area competitors and visible market positioning
- Search-intent differences by service line
- Seasonality and weather-related demand where relevant
- Housing stock, commercial mix, and other local conditions that may affect service demand

## Known variations that should prevent overgeneralization
- residential vs. commercial
- service/repair vs. new construction
- emergency-heavy vs. scheduled work
- owner-operator vs. multi-truck company
- geographic market and housing stock
- specialty services and equipment
- price positioning and service model

## Project-specific facts that must NOT be generalized

## Open research gaps
- Which customer decision patterns repeat across independent plumbing businesses?
- Which search and trust patterns are local-market effects rather than business-type effects?
- Which service categories create meaningfully different audience states and conversion paths?
